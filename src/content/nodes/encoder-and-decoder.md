An encoder is "any ML system that converts from a raw, sparse, or external
representation into a more processed, denser, or more internal representation."
A decoder does the reverse: "converts from a processed, dense, or internal
representation to a more raw, sparse, or external
representation."[[cite:google-glossary]]

In sequence terms: "an encoder takes an input sequence and returns an internal
state", and "a decoder starts with the internal state generated by the encoder
to predict the next sequence."[[cite:google-glossary]]

## Three shapes, three jobs

The [transformer](transformer) paper used both halves, for
translation.[[cite:attention-paper]] Google notes that later models did not have
to: "some Transformers pair encoders with decoders, though other Transformers
use only the encoder or only the decoder."[[cite:google-glossary]]

- **Encoder-only** — read something and produce a representation of it.
  Classification, retrieval, [embeddings](embedding). It sees the whole input at
  once, in both directions.
- **Decoder-only** — continue a sequence, one [token](token) at a time. This is
  what a [large language model](large-language-model) is, and it is why they are
  [autoregressive](autoregressive-model).
- **Encoder-decoder** — read one sequence, produce a different one. Translation
  and summarisation, where input and output are separate objects.

## Why decoder-only won

Because "continue this text" turned out to subsume the others. Translation,
summarisation and classification can all be phrased as continuation, given a
model good enough at continuing — which is what
[instruction tuning](instruction-tuning) then made reliable.

One architecture, one training objective, and every task expressed as a prompt.
The generality is the reason the encoder-decoder split, which looked
fundamental in 2017, is now mostly of historical and diagnostic interest.

## Where the encoder survives

In embedding models. Compressing a document into one vector is exactly the
encoder's job, and doing it with a decoder-only model is possible but awkward.

Which is worth knowing when choosing a model for
[semantic search](semantic-search): the best chat model is not the best
embedding model, and they are different shapes for a reason.
