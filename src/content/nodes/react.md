Before ReAct, reasoning and acting were separate research problems: "their
abilities for reasoning (e.g. chain-of-thought prompting) and acting (e.g.
action plan generation) have primarily been studied as separate
topics."[[cite:react-paper]]

The paper's move was to interleave them — generating "both reasoning traces and
task-specific actions in an interleaved manner" — and the reason it works is
stated as a two-way exchange: "reasoning traces help the model induce, track,
and update action plans as well as handle exceptions, while actions allow it to
interface with external sources, such as knowledge bases or environments, to
gather additional information."[[cite:react-paper]]

## Why each half needs the other

[Chain-of-thought prompting](chain-of-thought-prompting) reasons well and has
nothing but the model's own knowledge to reason over, so it cannot check
anything and drifts confidently when it is wrong.

Acting without reasoning executes a plan fixed before the first result came
back, which fails the moment reality differs from the assumption.

Interleaving means an observation can change the plan. That is the property that
turns a sequence of tool calls into something that can recover.

## The trace

Thought, action, observation — repeated. The alternation is what to look for
when identifying the pattern in a system's logs, and the observations are the
part that makes the trace worth reading, because they are the only entries not
generated by the model.[[cite:promptguide-react]]

## Why it is everywhere

Most [agent](agent) frameworks implement some version of this loop, often
without naming it. Modern [tool use](tool-use) APIs bake the structure into the
protocol: the model emits a [structured tool call](function-calling) rather than
text the harness must parse, and the result comes back as a typed message.

The prompting technique became a wire format, which is the usual sign that an
idea was right.

## What it does not solve

A wrong observation is still taken at face value — this is where
[indirect prompt injection](indirect-prompt-injection) enters an agent — and
nothing in the loop stops the model from reasoning its way around an obstacle in
a direction nobody wanted. ReAct makes recovery possible, not automatic.
